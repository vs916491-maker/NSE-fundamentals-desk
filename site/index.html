How ideas, themes and timing labels are worked out

Horizon: about one year. Only stocks with a market cap of ₹500 cr or more are considered.

Left out: companies with a loss over the last 12 months, no year-ago quarter to compare, results older than the previous quarter, or a red flag, rating downgrade or penalty of 0.5%+ of market cap in the last 180 days.

Score (out of 100):

  • Earnings, 40: latest quarter vs a year ago: sales (8, full at +20%) and net profit (12, full at +30%); the quarter before that, the same way (8); operating margin change (8, full at +2 pp; net margin for banks and lenders); profit not mostly other income or one-off items (4).
  • Orders, 15: order wins announced in the last 90 days as a share of the last 12 months' revenue (full at 30%). Single orders above 3× yearly revenue are ignored as misread amounts.
  • Ownership, 20: promoter stake change in the latest shareholding quarter (8; steady counts as good) and FII + DII stake change (12, full at +2 pp).
  • Institutional buying, 10: net buying by FIIs, DIIs and funds in bulk/block deals over 90 days, as % of market cap (full at 1%). Most large-cap buying never shows up as a bulk/block deal.
  • Valuation, 15: P/E (market cap ÷ 12-month profit) against profit growth, with growth capped at 40%: full marks when P/E ≤ growth, none when P/E ≥ 2.5× growth or above 80. Little growth: only a P/E under 25 scores, up to 6.

An idea needs 65+ and at least 3 of the 5 groups at half marks or better. "Close to qualifying" shows the next best (53+).

Themes group stocks by NSE industry (file collector/themes.csv). The theme score ranks themes against each other: share of companies with profit up 20%+ (35), order wins as % of revenue (25), average FII + DII stake change (25) and net institutional buying (15).

Price timing comes from Sector Strength (needs sign-in): Ready = relative-strength rating 70+ and above the 200-day average; Wait = below a falling 200-day average; Building = in between. It is not part of the score, so the track record can show whether waiting for "Ready" helps. A theme's stage comes from its Sector Strength index: Improving = Early, Leading = Established, Weakening or Lagging = Watch.

Track record: each idea is logged with the close on the day it first appeared and followed for a year, whether or not it stays an idea, against the Nifty 500 over the same dates.

Everything is worked out automatically from filings and can be wrong (order amounts especially). Open the filing and the chart before acting. Not investment advice.

How the verdicts are worked out

Each verdict adds up points from what FIIs changed over the period (3 sessions for short term, 10 for swing). Each change is compared with how much that same measure normally moves, so only unusual moves score:

  • Index futures positioning (weight 1.5): change in the share of FII index-futures contracts that are long.
  • Index futures buying (1): net ₹ cr bought or sold over the period.
  • Index options (1): change in net calls minus net puts. More call buying or put writing is bullish.
  • Stock futures (0.5): change in the long share of FII stock-futures positions.
  • Cash market (1): FII net buying in shares. Short term: ±₹1,500 cr = 1 point, ±₹5,000 cr = 2. Swing: ±₹4,000 / ±₹12,000 cr.

Each item scores −2 to +2, times its weight. Total ≥ +3 = Bullish, +1 to +3 = Mildly bullish, −1 to +1 = Neutral, −3 to −1 = Mildly bearish, ≤ −3 = Bearish.

FIIs also use index futures and puts to hedge the shares they own, so short positions are not always a bet on a fall. Treat the verdict as one input alongside price action, not a trade signal.

Change is versus the previous quarter's shareholding filing, in percentage points (pp). Filter applies to FII, DII, MF or promoter.

Bulk and block deals for the selected trading day. Institution type is a name-based guess: FII = foreign funds/FPIs, DII = Indian mutual funds, insurers, pension funds and banks, AIF/PMS = other Indian funds. High-frequency prop desks are left out.
