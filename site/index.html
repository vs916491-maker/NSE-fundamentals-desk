How the verdicts are worked out

Each verdict adds up points from what FIIs changed over the period (3 sessions for short term, 10 for swing). Each change is compared with how much that same measure normally moves, so only unusual moves score:

  • Index futures positioning (weight 1.5): change in the share of FII index-futures contracts that are long.
  • Index futures buying (1): net ₹ cr bought or sold over the period.
  • Index options (1): change in net calls minus net puts. More call buying or put writing is bullish.
  • Stock futures (0.5): change in the long share of FII stock-futures positions.
  • Cash market (1): FII net buying in shares. Short term: ±₹1,500 cr = 1 point, ±₹5,000 cr = 2. Swing: ±₹4,000 / ±₹12,000 cr.

Each item scores −2 to +2, times its weight. Total ≥ +3 = Bullish, +1 to +3 = Mildly bullish, −1 to +1 = Neutral, −3 to −1 = Mildly bearish, ≤ −3 = Bearish.

FIIs also use index futures and puts to hedge the shares they own, so short positions are not always a bet on a fall. Treat the verdict as one input alongside price action, not a trade signal.
